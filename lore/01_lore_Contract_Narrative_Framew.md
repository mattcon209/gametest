## Budget Bodyguards - Contract Narrative Framework

This document outlines the narrative framework for the daily contract system in Budget Bodyguards. It details story arcs (short and long term), character dialogues, mission objectives, and VIP personality integration.

**I. Overall Narrative Arc (Metanarrative)**

The players are employees of "Secure Solutions Inc.", a notoriously cheap bodyguard agency that specializes in protecting… unusual clients. The CEO, Reginald Worthing, is obsessed with efficiency and believes in pushing his employees to their absolute limit – hence the bungee cord setup and increasingly absurd contracts.  Players don't have individual storylines, but rather share an overarching narrative of escalating ridiculousness under Worthing’s management.

**A. Phase 1: “Breaking In” (Contracts 1-20)**
*   **Theme:** Establishing the chaos and core mechanics. New players learn the ropes – literally. Missions focus on basic hazard avoidance & VIP redirection. Narrative focuses on introducing Reginald's eccentric demands and Secure Solutions’ questionable reputation.
*   **Narrative Delivery:** Mostly through mission briefings from Reginald (see Dialogue Examples below). Minimal external character interaction.

**B. Phase 2: “Reputation Rising” (Contracts 21-50)**
*   **Theme:** Increasing complexity of hazards, VIP personalities start influencing gameplay more prominently. Introduction of recurring minor characters who become increasingly aware of SSI’s chaotic methods. The players begin to question Worthing's motives, but are too reliant on his contracts for income.
*   **Narrative Delivery:** Briefings from Reginald continue, interspersed with short radio transmissions or environmental storytelling (e.g., overheard conversations).

**C. Phase 3: "The Unraveling" (Contracts 51+)**
*   **Theme:**  Hazards become wildly unpredictable and dangerous. VIP personalities can significantly alter the entire contract dynamic. The recurring minor characters start actively interfering with Secure Solutions, leading to unexpected consequences during missions. A larger conspiracy surrounding Worthing and Secure Solutions may be hinted at.
*   **Narrative Delivery:** Reginald’s briefings become increasingly unhinged/desperate (potentially revealing snippets of a hidden agenda). More frequent radio chatter and NPC interactions with potential for choosing dialogue options influencing contract outcomes (minor benefits or drawbacks).



**II. Contract Objectives & VIP Personality Integration Examples**

Each daily contract will have three primary objectives:

1.  **Primary Objective:** Get the VIP to the Destination safely (required for completion)
2.  **Secondary Objective(s):** Tailored to the VIP personality and/or environmental conditions (optional, rewards bonus points).
3.  **Challenge Objective:** An optional, difficult objective that offers substantial rewards but significantly increases risk.

**A. VIP Personality Examples & Contract Objectives:**

*   **The Diva:**
    *   Secondary: Prevent ANY stains on the VIP’s clothing (+50 Points)
    *   Challenge: Guide the VIP through a muddy puddle *without* getting them dirty (+100 points, -20% contract payout if failed)
*   **The Glass Cannon:**
    *   Secondary: Avoid all direct physical contact with obstacles and NPCs. (+30 Points)
    *   Challenge: Successfully navigate a crowd of aggressive pigeons without the VIP taking any damage. (+75 Points, Significant risk of early contract failure).
*   **The Paranoid:**
     *  Secondary: Keep the VIP calm by preventing sudden movements or loud noises (Maintain low stress level (+40 points)
    * Challenge: Successfully navigate a fireworks display while maintaining low paranoia. (-15% Contract Payout if failed).


**III. Character Dialogue Examples (Reginald Worthing – CEO, Secure Solutions Inc.)**

These are delivered as mission briefings at the start of each daily contract rotation. Tone is condescending, overly optimistic, and focused on cost-cutting.

*   **(Contract 1):**  "Alright team! New contract secured! Protecting Ms. Periwinkle—a prize-winning hamster – to her flower show. Simple enough. Minimal expenditure required.  Don't let that furry friend get a single speck of dust on her, or I’m docking your pay! Go, go!"
*   **(Contract 15):** "Excellent work last cycle! We’re pushing you all harder now – protection detail for Bartholomew 'The Baron' Buttersworth – a sentient topiary sculpture. Ensure he remains pristine and in polite conversation *at all times*. He dislikes being jostled." (followed by maniacal chuckle).
*   **(Contract 35):** "Right then! This one’s vital, people!  Protecting Professor Quentin Quibble—a brilliant but easily startled inventor—as he demonstrates his new 'Anti-Gravity Tea Kettle'. Keep him calm and ensure *no one interferes with the kettle*. Failure is not an option… or at least, is a financially unpleasant option. "
*   **(Contract 50+):** (Voice wavering slightly) “...Alright team... This is extremely important. Protect… Agnes - A time-displaced housecat from the future. She's... fragile and VERY sensitive to temporal paradoxes." *static crackle* "...Complete discretion requested. And for goodness sake, try not to break anything.”



**IV. Environmental Storytelling Examples**

*   Overheard radio transmissions mentioning Secure Solutions' poor reputation.
*   Graffiti on walls: "Secure Solutions - Don’t get caught!"
*   Newspaper clippings hinting at Worthing's dubious dealings.
*   Brief, panicked messages left by previous SSI employees.



This framework is intended to be flexible and adaptable to ensure a constant flow of fresh and engaging content for the daily contract system.
